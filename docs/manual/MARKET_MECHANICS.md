## The Live Data Feed

Crypto Survivors is not a traditional game. The enemies, the difficulty, and even your weapons are physically hardwired to the live Binance WebSockets. What happens in the real-world Bitcoin market happens to you in the Arena, instantly.

There are three primary market indicators you must monitor on your HUD.

## 1. RSI (Relative Strength Index)

RSI measures the momentum of the market. It tells you if the market is Overbought (greedy) or Oversold (fearful).

- **Oversold (RSI < 30)**: The market is crashing. 
  - **Effect**: Enemy spawn rates decrease, but enemies become desperate and move faster. The arena turns into a survival horror scenario.
- **Neutral (RSI 30-70)**: Standard market conditions.
  - **Effect**: Normal spawn rates and enemy behaviors.
- **Overbought (RSI > 70)**: The market is pumping. 
  - **Effect**: Massive swarms of enemies spawn, but they are slower and easier to herd. This is your chance to farm massive amounts of experience.

## 2. ATR (Average True Range)

ATR measures volatility. It tells you how violently the price is swinging up and down.

- **Low Volatility**: The market is flat and boring.
  - **Effect**: Enemy damage is normal. Bosses are less likely to spawn.
- **High Volatility (ATR Spikes)**: Chaos in the market.
  - **Effect**: **DANGER.** Enemy damage multipliers skyrocket. Elite enemies and bosses have a much higher chance to breach the arena. However, your critical hit chance also increases during these chaotic periods.

## 3. Trade PnL (Profit and Loss)

When you start a run, the system automatically opens a simulated "Long" or "Short" position on Bitcoin at the current exact price.

- **Positive PnL (In the Green)**: Your prediction was correct. The price moved in your favor.
  - **Effect**: Your weapons deal bonus damage, and enemies drop higher-tier experience gems.
- **Negative PnL (In the Red)**: The market turned against you.
  - **Effect**: Your damage output is reduced. You must rely on dodging and survival tactics until the market recovers.
